Expected-points model · season

Does the projection add up as a team?

The model projects every player on his own. Nothing in it forces a team's numbers to be possible — so the test is whether they are. Every passing yard is somebody's receiving yard, every pass attempt is somebody's target, and a team has seventeen games of quarterback to give.

The identities

must hold by definition

These are not estimates that ought to be close — they are the same events counted from two different ends of the model, built from different players' histories. If they disagree, the parts do not fit together.

Through the fixtures

the end-to-end test

The hardest check available: turn the summed player projections back into team scoring, run all 272 fixtures, and compare the resulting spreads against the ones the books have actually posted. Defence is not modelled here at all — it is proxied from last season's points allowed, regressed — so this measures the offence and little else.

The market on whole teams

season win totals, four books

The yardstick the fixture test was missing. VegasInsider publishes season win totals across four books — and they post different lines for the same team, so the page cannot be averaged. Baltimore came back at o11.5 (+120) from one book and o10.5 (−150) from another. Each quote is converted to the total it implies before anything is combined; the two Baltimore numbers then land within 0.01 of each other.

The market on individual players

what the books price, scored our way

Season over/unders per player from BettingPros, scored under this league's rules into the board's MKT column. It is not the same quantity as the projection beside it — a line already discounts the games the books expect a player to miss, and the model assumes a full season on purpose — so the two are shown together and never blended.
